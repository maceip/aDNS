# Historical tiny-zone CCF and stock BIND baseline, 2026-09-13

This historical baseline records a local CCF primary and an unmodified BIND secondary that completed an idle DNSSEC refresh test, authenticated transfer checks, and a concurrent fixed-load measurement. The primary used CCF's explicit `Virtual` test platform. These results exercise real CCF transactions, its private KV maps, consensus callbacks, enclave-side DNSSEC and TSIG code, and stock BIND; they do not establish confidential hardware execution, a native attested service registration, or Azure performance.

The measured primary is the immutable image recorded in [build.json](evidence/ccf-bind-local-20260913/build.json): AMD64 manifest `sha256:ebb2f0ae114744cc59255945088b7fb87189de467cbed98036ed445d107b9c7c`, executable SHA-256 `be6131ec2cec3434ad3e4e57f51fa807fbe13cdec80fd061515dc99ae1399b2a`. It remained unchanged throughout the measurements. The subsequent contract candidate, executable `7b41a3a9c147a923542bdda424d9c669f0d166d5c5c8601934b164928218e888`, is separately identified and tested in [the CCF build record](evidence/ccf/build.json). It includes subsequent DS routing, effective lifecycle status, ACME grant rechecks, supervisor deadline fixes, complete-RRset TTL normalization, durable request observations, TSIG identity revocation and immutable appraisal-policy IDs; this older measurement must not be attributed to that new binary.

The primary and BIND ran in separate AMD64 Docker containers sharing a private network namespace on a local Apple-silicon Docker host. CCF HTTPS was CA-pinned and used HTTP/1.1. BIND transferred over TSIG-authenticated TCP to a loopback relay on port 5353, which forwarded packets through the CCF private interface. BIND alone answered the independent validator's ordinary DNS queries on port 53. The validator/load generator ran as an ARM64 container on the Docker bridge. The zone had three unsigned source records (SOA, NS, A), 16 records in its signed transfer, signature validity 600 seconds and refresh interval 300 seconds. There were no successful registration, governance, or record mutations during the idle/load windows. Four denied owner-scope nonce requests and read-only checks occurred.

The load interval was 01:39:05.600–01:59:05.947 UTC. This was fixed offered load on a shared Docker host, not a maximum-throughput test or isolated benchmark. Policy generation, fixture and Valgrind tests, Rust checks, and the revised image build shared that host during the window. Latency percentiles describe the independent successful samples; both their failure count and dnsperf packet loss are reported above. RSS describes one actual Linux process in each container, excludes other processes, and does not establish absence of memory leaks. Signing durations cover `SignedZone::sign_with_keys`, including denial and signature generation, and exclude preceding reads, subsequent storage writes, and consensus. Four spans are insufficient to characterize a broad signing-time distribution.

The independent verifier authenticated a real CCF receipt binding `example.test.` to its complete KSK DNSKEY RDATA, owner, algorithm, key tag and DS digest. Only after verification did it produce the trust anchor used by `delv`. The live TSIG transfer verifier authenticated every message, compared the transferred KSK with that receipt, validated the full zone with `ldns-verify-zone`, and checked that BIND was authoritative at the same serial. Unsigned and wrong-key transfer requests were rejected. `delv` validated SOA, DNSKEY, apex TXT NODATA, and absent-name NXDOMAIN responses. The tightened bounded-transfer check independently repeated these checks at serial 10.

An initial startup ordering issue was observed before the measurement: the helper attempted NOTIFY/SOA work before BIND listened, received connection refusal, and retried after its 300-second outstanding-work timeout. BIND's own initial authenticated AXFR succeeded immediately at serial 7; subsequent refreshes and every observed status sample succeeded. Starting BIND before the helper does not eliminate the race with its initial AXFR. Readiness must wait for a successful authenticated observation, including the bounded initial retry when necessary. The complete BIND transfer log preserves the actual timings rather than assuming notification implies propagation.

The manifest hashes each exported artifact. Ledger files, recovery shares, member keys, TLS keys, TSIG secrets, bearer tokens, and private configuration are excluded.

Real CCF smoke, three-node quorum loss, and member-share disk recovery passed against executable `7b41a3a9c147a923542bdda424d9c669f0d166d5c5c8601934b164928218e888`.
